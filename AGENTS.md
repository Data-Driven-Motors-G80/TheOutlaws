# TheOutlaws project guidance

Applies throughout this repository. Read before making changes. The user's latest request takes priority.

## Product decisions — level play (current)
- The home screen uses illustrated, responsive destination cards (`WorldShiftMenuPresentation`, art in `Assets/Resources/WorldShiftLevelCards`) with clear next/completed/locked states. It is a level select: 0 Training, 1 City, 2 Ocean, 3 Mountain, 4 Snow, 5 Desert (ordered by difficulty: both Hard levels last). Each card shows its difficulty as 1-3 chili peppers, and a beaten level gets a green COMPLETED! wash; it stays playable. Every level is open from the start (Drew, 2026-11-23: no waiting on another level, no unlock text). Beaten levels are saved per level in PlayerPrefs (`WorldShift.Levels.BeatenMask`; older highest-beaten saves carry over).
- Resolve menu canvas/grid layout before the first draw and after viewport changes. `WorldShiftSceneLoading` provides immediate feedback while scenes and music load asynchronously; block repeated navigation and hold driving/audio until the loading surface is gone. Navigation tests wait for actual readiness, not a fixed number of frames.
- Team testing: Shift+U on the home screen, or **The Outlaws > Levels > Unlock All Levels (testing)** in the Editor, opens every level without changing saved progress. **Reset Level Progress** clears it.
- Every level runs in `City_WorldShift`; `WorldShiftLevelCourse` decides the environment and layout. New levels need course data, not a new scene.
- Core mechanic: still obstacles belong to Day or Night. The other world's still obstacles always show as dashed outlines (Day = yellow, Night = black). Dashes are passable; switching while inside one is a crash. Oncoming traffic belongs to one world and also shows as dashes.
- Anything that moves sideways (sliders, desert rocks) or rolls toward you (rolling rocks) exists in both worlds, keeps one orange colour, moves in Day and freezes in Night. Ocean airborne dolphins cross continuously in Day and freeze in Night, even before the player approaches.
- Obstacle visual rules for every level live in `Docs/OBSTACLE_VISUAL_RULES.md`: one-world (swappable) obstacles show the sun/moon panel (`WorldShiftSnowObstacleArt.WorldPanel`), sideways movers show the shared arrow (`WorldShiftMoverArrow`), no text on obstacles or the road, sideways movers move whenever it is Day (no waking up as you approach). Read it before changing obstacles; its "Open issues" list tracks levels not yet compliant.
- After a successful level landing, the short recovery deck joins both worlds and Space switches immediately. The landing stays world-specific until touchdown succeeds; a retry before that ramp resets its recovery deck.
- One hit = crash. The police car (otherwise a fixed distance behind) pulls up to you, then you restart at the last checkpoint. No lives, pursuit gap or police meter.
- Level wins save/unlock immediately, then `WorldShiftLevelEscape` plays a 6.5-second visual escape: Ocean boards a departing ferry while the police stop at the dock; City/Snow/Mountain lose pursuit through themed closing service passages. Desert ends the campaign with a 14-second passage escape and dawn overlook, followed by three skippable comic panels closing the stolen-device story and a full-screen THE END with level select/replay. The preserved finale artwork and dawn overlook now play after Desert. Its original finale chord waits for THE END. Skip / Esc / Enter can skip the drive after .65 s; a later Continue enters the next level. `WorldShiftLevelFinish` shows “You lost them,” the next destination and replay/menu actions. Training uses “Ready for the getaway” without a chase cinematic. Victory audio waits for the reveal; the siren fades away. Keep the cinematic separate from driving simulation and clean up its camera, sky, visuals and audio on scene exit.
- City (Level 1) opens with a short, skippable three-panel comic about the stolen Day/Night device, the alarm and the police chase. Show it on a fresh City selection or after Training; bypass it on restarts and checkpoint retries. Hold driving, chase audio and the attempt timer until Start getaway or Skip. Art: `Assets/Resources/WorldShiftStory/CityIntro.png`; presentation: `WorldShiftLevelIntro.cs`. Keep captions minimal and match the illustrated menu/pause style.
- Ocean opens with a 5.5-second in-engine helicopter fly-in, reusing the coast and real aircraft. The City story gate holds driving/audio/attempt time, and Skip / Enter / Esc starts play immediately. It also starts automatically at the end; full retries and checkpoint retries bypass the intro. Presentation: WorldShiftOceanIntro.cs and WorldShiftOceanHelicopter.Intro.cs. Keep City comic behavior intact.
- Levels have 5 sections, except Desert (Level 5), which has 6 including a dedicated sandstorm rock field. Every section ends with a big ramp: launch in one world, switch midair, land in the other. Landings are normally checkpoints; Desert instead has 7 respawn positions including the start, concentrated before difficult ramps and the storm. Training uses plain gates for its first four steps.
- Desert launches in Day, Night, Day, Night, Day, Day. Night flights have a full-width vulture flock in the later half (the airborne sliding block). Blocks swing evenly side to side in Day, 4.5 m each way (kept close to the road) and freeze in Night; six ground blocks (camel caravans) use the same rule, including two in the storm. Every Desert sideways mover moves whenever it is Day, wherever the player is (`ShiftHazard.MovesWheneverDay`). The final Day jump has 10 fast spheres sweeping all three lanes in two waves with widely staggered, shuffled phases and a steering window; depth spacing prevents overlap. The storm has 19 ground rocks. Desert air steering uses .65 control. All movers stay orange and solid in both worlds. Dust fades before ramps and retains at least 70 m of road visibility.
- Desert scenery uses low wind-shaped dune meshes with no colliders, clear of all road bends; retain the road-following sand/seam fix. Desert obstacles follow Snow's format (`WorldShiftDesertObstacleArt`, `WorldShiftDesertRockArt`, `WorldShiftDesertCreatures`): wide Day/Night closures alternate sandstone hoodoos and tall saguaro cactus stands, both with sun/moon panels (cacti are static blockers only, never movers). Sideways movers must be things that really move sideways, and floating things must be living flying animals: sliders are camels crossing the road, ground sliding blocks are camel caravans, ground spheres are tumbleweeds, airborne spheres are large flapping vultures (hitbox unchanged), airborne sliding blocks are vulture flocks. Animals face their direction of travel and animate on the Day clock. No circles under obstacles; flying animals have no ice ring. No text: no DAY TO MOVE, no STORM road paint. The dust field has three alternating Night-only half-road barriers.
- Desert ordinary driving uses alternating 80 m canyon bends (about 28–32 degrees), with a gentler S-bend in the storm. Jump approaches remain straight from ramp minus 80 m through recovery end plus 45 m; road-piece boundaries align there. Both worlds share the road geometry.
- Removed from play: cash, garage/upgrades, manual shooting/ammo (Ocean now has optional auto-fire rockets), road splitting (forks), cash-out exits, the optional bonus ramp. Disabled (code kept for later levels): fuel and power-ups. The disconnected rift visual has been removed.
- Typography uses bundled Barlow Medium/Bold for controls and readable UI, with Barlow Condensed Semibold for display headings. `WorldShiftTypography` applies the hierarchy; TextMesh Pro defaults to the bundled UI font. Training sign artwork uses the same source fonts via `Tools/Art/generate_training_signs.swift`. Keep the SIL OFL license with the files in `Assets/Fonts/Barlow`; do not ship system-only fonts.
- Training introduces Day/Night switching once, with a sign showing Space and a raised barrier lowering flush into the road. The next section is uninterrupted practice, with no separate dashes sign. The traffic lesson is a Day-only car with the sun roof badge: steer round it, or switch to Night and it becomes passable dashes (it never freezes; freezing is taught next). Five signs map explicitly to checkpoint sections 0, 1, 3, 4 and 5; keep the existing six sections, checkpoints, freeze lesson and final jump.
- Use as little text as possible. Training teaches with green highway signs (key caps + arrows/pictograms). Instructional green highway signs are exclusive to Training; do not place them in regular levels. Level checkpoints have green road stripes, a labeled gate and a brief CHECKPOINT confirmation above the car's sight line. The rest of the HUD uses sun/moon, progress and pause icons.
- City streetlights have inward-facing warm pools, small depth-tested bulb halos and textured asphalt highlights. `WorldShiftLocalLighting` supplies at most eight nearby local lights to shared stylized surfaces, so lamps/headlights also illuminate cars and scenery. Keep glare restrained, switch lamp glow off in Day and clear shader light inputs on scene cleanup. Streetlight dressing lives in `WorldShiftStreetLighting.cs`; no extra gameplay colliders or road geometry changes.
- City uses the original warm palette with framed windows, podiums, shop fronts, pavement joints, planters and CityStreetlight prefabs. Desert has wind-shaped dunes and coconut-palm oases with reeds, shore stones and calm water. Both use a shared gradient sky with distinct Day/Night palettes. Scenery has no gameplay colliders, is checked against the whole road and follows its height; shoulders reuse exact road-edge vertices and never bridge gaps. Training uses the collider-free practice depot in `WorldShiftTrainingScenery.cs`: hangars, loading bays, cones, fences, trees and Night floodlights stay outside the lesson lanes and clear of the final jump. Training "driving academy" dressing (`WorldShiftTrainingLife.cs`, visual only, animated on the street clock): control towers with spinning radar, beacon, windsock and flags; coned slalom loops with a practice car and a cycling traffic light; skid pads with a drifting car and a camera drone; red/white tyre walls, wordless practice signs and checkered water towers. No people in Training (people are City-only). The jump pit is a blue stunt airbag with hazard-striped lips. Keep it lively but calm, never in the lanes or near the final jump. Steering signs and enlarged previews teach both A/D and arrow keys; keep all fallback instructions specific to the current lesson.
- Level crashes show an English retry card: BUSTED for collisions, MISSED LANDING / OFF THE ROAD for falls, MISSED DIAMOND for collection misses. Keep the existing automatic checkpoint retry timing.
- Art and assets are now allowed. Ocean has a stylized coastal art pass in `WorldShiftOceanPresentation`, finish/landmark/shadow details in `WorldShiftOceanPolish`, and its compact HUD in `WorldShiftOceanHudPolish`. Keep prominent world-coloured hazard panels, world-switch silhouettes and road visibility clear.
- All current levels, including Training, use the same maroon player coupe (`WorldShiftPlayerCarArt`) and marked police cruiser (`WorldShiftPoliceCarArt`). Ocean adds lighting/contact shadows without replacing either model.
- Pursuit cars use `WorldShiftPoliceCarArt`: black-and-white panels, POLICE livery and alternating red/blue roof beacons with a distance-attenuated siren (`WorldShiftPoliceEmergencyEffects`). Effects pause with the game and stopped Training lessons, and stop when pursuit ends. Ocean must retain this shared cruiser rather than replace it with its player coupe.
- Level audio uses `WorldShiftLevelAudio`: shared engine/tyres/jump/landing/crash/victory cues, with Ocean-only ambience and distinct licensed Kevin MacLeod songs in all five environments (Training stays quiet); Night softens the current song without restarting it. Pause has saved Music and Effects sliders (`WorldShiftAudioSettings`); effects volume includes existing chimes, horns and siren. Audio pauses during stopped Training lessons. Original effects generation lives in `Tools/Audio/generate_game_audio.py`. Licensed song credits/source URLs live in `Assets/Resources/WorldShiftMusic/CREDITS.txt` and Pause > Music credits. Keep the siren subtle (.04 max, 1600 Hz low-pass).
- Ocean checkpoints use `WorldShiftOceanCheckpoints`: four coastal gantries at actual saves, green stripes and reflective accents, with one-time light/chime confirmation. Keep supports outside the road and the chequered finish separate.
- Ocean (Level 2) has 12 optional Day-only rocket pickups. Each auto-fires a detailed, 1.35-second arcing homing rocket at the police helicopter (6 hits to defeat; combinations weaken at 3 and 5); health visuals wait for the hit. Night freezes warning/missile clocks; landed debris stays solid. There are 20 authored attack starts (3/4/4/4/5), including a 108 m spread-point corridor in each section. Spread corridors replace two compact slots, with no ordinary hazards or ammo inside. Their seven impacts are 18 m apart across all lanes; later launch/impact times follow road distance. Compact seven-point patterns also remain. Each 1.8 m square has independent collision after impact. At 1 HP, subsequent ordinary slots repeatedly fire 18-missile barrages until a kill; compact volleys retain an outside exit, long volleys alternate exits across three six-missile waves 54 m apart. The saved salvo flag is history, not a one-shot limit. Five slow missile-curtain opportunities (one per section, at most five while alive) fire 15 near-full-width missiles with 3.05–3.35 s impact timing and 90 m warning; Night freezing is their route. Damage removes cargo/twins, not slot frequency. All attacks cancel on a kill. Whole start/end spans are checked, and attacks expire after the final target, not the first row. Night closures stay at least 28 m beyond attack-pocket edges; jumps, saves and ammo stay clear. Full-health singles/twins can combine with Day cargo, retracted with sun icons and .18 s animation; point fields/curtains add no cargo. Single/twin aim mixes current lane with .35-.55 s bounded steering prediction, using a per-run seed preserved by retries; markers lock immediately and never retarget. Ordinary Ocean driving also has 15 Day barrier rows and ten original teal police spike sliders (two per section at offsets 124/347 m); reuse WorldShiftSpikePlatform without model/palette changes. Later compact attack pockets shift +8 m to clear the second spike. Selected final closures belong to Day, with 34 m of clear ramp approach; all authored helicopter slots remain safe. Ten oncoming cars use shared sun/moon roof plates (Snow retains its existing panel). Ocean flights are 132 m with .6 air steering. Each jump has two fast, broadside giant dolphins at takeoff +38/+98 m. Both exist in both worlds: at centre they span all lanes, Day opens a side passage, Night freezes the opening. Tapered collision follows the body; all upcoming dolphins move throughout Day, without a proximity wake, and retry resets motion. Defeat gives an air burst, then a fixed world-space dive into the sea, splash/foam, crash audio and a second short decaying camera kick. Dolphin/missile deaths show short English mechanic hints and a 3-second retry; other crashes keep their old timing. Ammo and boss damage bank only when actually collected before a save. Missing ammo never fails; all effects reset on retry. See Docs/LEVEL3_OCEAN.md.
- Snow (Level 4) is the black-ice level: five sections, each with one long ice stretch (`LevelCourse.IceZones`, drawn by `WorldShiftIcePatches`). On ice the car keeps its forward speed, but steering builds a slide slowly, letting go keeps sliding and only countersteering brakes it (`WorldShiftCity.Ice.cs`); bends push a sliding car wide. Snow is meant to be genuinely hard: 85+ obstacles, all of them steering or Space timing (barricades, one-world walls and traffic, zippers where barricades leave one lane whose block flips world every 24 m). Do not add obstacles that only move in Day (sliders, sliding gates, rolling boulders) to Snow; Drew finds them unfair. Rules (enforced by `WorldShiftLevelTests.Snow.cs`): ice starts at least 40 m after a landing and ends at least 70 m before the next ramp; a Day obstacle and a Night obstacle never share a spot (at least 20 m apart, traffic counted where it meets you), so switching always reveals a different layout; a scripted keyboard-only driver must clear every section. Re-run that test after any Snow layout change. Ice is Snow-only for now.
- Mountain (Level 3), "Rockslide Pass", is **medium-hard** (playtesters found a 35-obstacle course too easy; 53 was too hard): 74 obstacles (Drew, 2026-11-23: make it a little harder than Ocean), with faster boulders and goats; sections end with runs of full walls alternating worlds 36 m apart (the shared 35 m minimum switch window; three in section 1, four in section 2), sections 3 and 4 add a quick Day/Night pair mid-section, and extra boulders roll down the lanes you are not using. Section lengths (and so bends, grade and guardrail gaps) are unchanged. Boulder slides follow forced lane changes, Night walls come in quick pairs that force Day (restarting the slide), and two goats cross back to back in opposite directions. Rolling boulders (`Rock`, drawn as boulders with no sideways arrow, rolling in Day and frozen in Night) come down the road; Night rockfall walls force switches to Day that restart the slide. Goats (`Crossing`; `WorldShiftMountainGoat`, white mountain goats and alpine ibex alternating, trotting and facing their direction of travel over the unchanged 1 m sphere) cross the road whenever it is Day; cliff ledges (half walls) force a side. No sliders, sliding blocks or traffic. Three 24 m guardrail gaps (`LevelCourse.EdgeGaps`) on the valley (right) side of strong left bends: the car may leave the road there and a fall past the road edge is OFF THE ROAD and a checkpoint retry; holding the outer lane is safe, the rail holds the car everywhere else. Gaps stay on the outside of bends, clear of jumps, respawns and goat crossings, and (by playtest request, a deliberate loosening of fairness) each sits beside an obstacle that forces the right lane, so dodging means passing the drop. Gaps are marked to read from far away: tall self-lit red-and-white chevron boards with flashing amber beacons at both ends and 30 m before, torn rail ends, and red-and-white kerb paint along the edge. Background life (`WorldShiftMountainSkyLife.cs`, visual only): aerial cable cars crossing the valley well above the road and bald eagles soaring in banked circles; pause stops them. Day/Night closures are rockslide piles (`WorldShiftMountainObstacleArt`) with the sun/moon panel. Finishing touches (`WorldShiftMountainDressing.cs`, visual only): valley mist below the road on the drop side, waterfalls on a few uphill cuttings, picture-only falling-rock signs, chalets with lit windows and stone road markers; a stone-and-timber checkpoint gate; a Mountain Night sky (`_Mountain` in `WorldShiftEnvironmentSky`: big moon, Milky Way, twinkling stars); and wind, eagle cries and goat bleats (`WorldShiftLevelAudio.Mountain.cs`, clips from `Tools/Audio/generate_mountain_audio.py`). Every jump has a big bald eagle (`WorldShiftMountainEagle`, `Builder.Eagle`, about a 10 m wingspan, wings level like a soaring eagle, mostly gliding with short bursts of wingbeats) gliding across the flight, two on the summit jump (Mountain's answer to Desert's flyers, at medium difficulty: one per jump, because weak air steering cannot slalom between several). Eagles glide whenever it is Day and freeze in Night: off a Day ramp switch to Night in the air to freeze them, off a Night ramp they stay frozen until you switch to Day. Hitting one is a crash and a checkpoint retry (`WorldShiftCity` checks airborne collisions in Desert and Mountain; unchanged 1.1 m sphere); the shared arrow, no ring, and a close eagle cry at takeoff. Goats keep the shared ice block when frozen; eagles have no frozen cue (they stop gliding; playtesters preferred that to a frosted look). Section 4 has a goat herd: three goats in single file 5 m apart, so there is always a side to pass. The road climbs and descends up to 8.5% (`LevelCourse.MountainGrade`); the car cruises slower uphill and faster downhill (`GradeSpeedFactor`, never on jump approaches, which stay flat). One-time HUD hints (`WorldShiftLevelHud.IceHint.cs`, shared with Snow's ice hint) explain goats and guardrail gaps the first time you meet them. Driving off a gap (`WorldShiftCity.GapFall.cs`): the car carries its speed straight off the edge, tips over the break of the slope and rolls downhill toward the mist while the camera follows; the OFF THE ROAD card waits about a second, and the jump fall burst is skipped. Jumps are ski jumps (`WorldShiftRelayVisuals.Mountain.cs`: timber walls with red-and-white kerbs, a hazard-striped lip and pennant poles, launch world only; boost strip and chevrons unchanged) over a deep, clean misty gorge (`WorldShiftLevelDropScenery.Mountain.cs`, theme "Mountain misty gorge"). The win escape is its own scene: the coupe drives into a stone-arched tunnel and an avalanche gate drops on the police (`WorldShiftLevelEscape.Mountain.cs`). Music: Kevin MacLeod's "Comin Round the Mountain". Rules and a scripted keyboard-only driver live in `WorldShiftLevelTests.Mountain.cs`; re-run them after any Mountain layout change.
- Analytics: `WorldShiftLevelAnalytics` writes one `ws_level_attempts` row per attempt and `wl_*` events (see Docs/ANALYTICS.md, "Levels").

## Faster working process
- For requests for advice or lists, give recommendations without changing gameplay unless asked.
- Make the smallest coherent change. Use the map below instead of repeatedly exploring the whole project.
- Batch related edits before launching Unity. Run checks once on the finished change; rerun only when a failure or subsequent change justifies it.
- For documentation and wording-only changes, use a diff review. Do not launch Unity or run a full gameplay suite by default.
- For layout changes, inspect one representative rendered view when practical. For mechanics, run the closest relevant tests first.
- Broaden testing only for shared driving/collision/world-state changes, scene-flow changes or unresolved risks. Do not routinely rerun every suite for each small UI edit.
- Reuse the current Unity session when supported. Do not close the user's Editor or launch a second Unity instance against the same project.
- If an isolated test copy is needed, reuse an existing one and sync only changed files. Never assume its source or results are current. Avoid repeatedly copying Library or creating fresh projects for small changes.
- The temporary test copy used in this session is `/tmp/outlaws-balance-check`. If another run occupies it, `/tmp/outlaws-finale-check` is an existing alternative. Check the chosen copy exists and matches the relevant source before using it.
- Unity version comes from `ProjectSettings/ProjectVersion.txt` (currently `6000.3.23f1`). Use targeted `-testFilter` values and save results/logs under `/tmp`.
- `--burst-disable-compilation` has avoided a native Burst startup crash in the temporary test runner. This is a test-run flag, not a project-setting change.
- Report exactly what was checked and what remains unverified. Keep progress updates and final responses brief.
- Preserve unrelated working-tree changes, local Unity account links, editor-tool settings and material serialization noise. Do not stage them incidentally.
- PR descriptions should be casual bullet lists about mechanics and player-visible behavior, with brief validation; avoid implementation detail dumps.

## Where to look
- `README.md` and `Docs/README.md` are the current entry points. `Docs/Archive` is historical, and `Docs/Legacy` describes the older World game. Do not treat historical mechanics as current requirements.
- `Assets/Editor/WorldShiftCampaignSetup.cs` creates only the menu and shared gameplay scene without overwriting existing scenes. Keep both scene paths and `.meta` GUIDs stable. Retired scene tools and tests live outside Assets in `Docs/Archive/RetiredScenes`.
- `Assets/Scripts/WorldShift/WorldShiftCity.cs`: simulation, controls, collisions, fuel and world switching. `DesertTrialRock.cs`: shared rolling-rock motion. Driving (`AutoDriveCar`) and the police follower (`ChaseCar`) are finite-route only; the old World game code has been removed.
- `WorldShiftFlow.cs`: five-environment cycle and cash-out cadence. `WorldShiftEscapeProfile.cs`: Ocean/Snow/Mountain courses, fuel, relays and colours, plus City difficulty scaling. `WorldShiftGetaway.cs` in that directory: escape loop, cash and HUD rendering. `WorldShiftDrivingMessages.cs`: gameplay prompt priority, shooting hint pacing and Nightshift reminders.
- `WorldShiftHud.cs`: HUD for the legacy standalone practice run. `WorldShiftModeGuide.cs`: shared mode names/copy.
- `WorldShiftRoute.cs`, `WorldShiftForkRoad.cs`, `WorldShiftRelay.cs`, `WorldShiftRelayVisuals.cs`: roads, choices, gaps and landings.
- `WorldShiftCombat.cs`: shared obstacle active-state check for targeting, collision, steering and rendering. `WorldShiftGetawayExtras.cs`: shield, magnet, boost and optional bonus ramp. `WorldShiftObstacleVisual.cs`: consistent X blocks. `WorldShiftBlockVisuals.cs`: player and police blocks. `WorldShiftPickupVisual.cs`: shared labeled supply blocks.
- `WorldShiftPresentation.cs`: environment and hazard visuals.
- `Assets/Tests/Editor/WorldShiftSceneSetupTests.cs`: the two current scenes and build setup. `WorldShiftLevelTests*.cs` (partial class): core rules in the main file, plus `.MenuFlow`, `.Training`, `.Ocean`, `.Audio` and `.Presentation` — filter by test name for targeted runs. `DesertLevelCourseTests.cs`: current Level 5. `WorldShiftRelayTests.cs`: shared jumps.
- Level play: `WorldShiftLevels.cs` (unlocks, flow), `WorldShiftLevelCourse.cs` (all level layouts), `WorldShiftLevelHud.cs`, `WorldShiftLevelProps.cs` (dashes, highway signs), `WorldShiftIcons.cs`, `WorldShiftLevelAnalytics.cs`. Level-mode branches in `WorldShiftCity` are guarded by `LevelMode`. Tests: `Assets/Tests/Editor/WorldShiftLevelTests*.cs`.
- `ProjectSettings/EditorBuildSettings.asset`: only `WorldShift_Menu`, followed by `City_WorldShift`. Training and all five levels share the latter scene.
